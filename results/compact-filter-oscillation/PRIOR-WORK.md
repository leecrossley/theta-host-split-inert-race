# Prior work and scope

The qualitative argument combines older tools. Its field-specific conclusions concern arbitrary compact signed prime weights on both trace hosts, the growth of the portion of the zero spectrum that survives a given weight, and numerical bounds against all affine predictors of the stated lengths. The estimates on the perfect-power meshes use continuous starting parameters $`n^k`$ indexed by integers $`n`$. The dyadic coordinates within an observation are read at the common scales $`n^k,2n^k,\ldots`$.

## Uniform windows and prime-power observations

The fixed-window result combines compactly supported tests, Dirichlet zero density and existing methods for oscillation. The new statements here are the equivalence between independence of arbitrary continuous compact weights and interior convex reach in **every** multiplicative window of a fixed size on these two hosts, the sharp count of observation starts on polynomially dense grids for regular weights, and a single deadline over all predictions in the numerical tables. The field and the chosen list of weights, or the fixed observation mesh, may enter the deadline.

Kaczorowski and Pintz locate oscillations for arithmetic functions with singular Mellin transforms, including prime counts in progressions. Montgomery and Vorhauer study fixed-factor sign changes of the ordinary Chebyshev error under a Riemann-hypothesis assumption. Schlage-Puchta treats large values of the ordinary prime-number-theorem remainder arising from a zero. Rubinstein and Sarnak, Martin and Ng, and Fiorilli and Jouve investigate distributions and races, including Chebotarev races and hypotheses on their component functions. A family of weighted scores, an exact convex average of common observations on a fixed prime-power grid, and one deadline uniform over a sphere of predictors are different conclusions. The finite line brackets and all 28 numerical radii first appeared in version 1.0.0. Its forecast counterexamples occurred arbitrarily late, and its original sparse-grid argument could follow each fixed projection. Earlier work in this family supplied a common fixed window for the two particular fields' dyadic levels and moves together, and a compact positive average with a signed interpolation test for a single growing zero. The present proof takes a finite list of unrestricted weights on either field, kills shifted half-strip spectra while retaining chosen critical-line coefficients, and groups original coefficients by their real parts to control rounding uniformly in changing projection directions. Classical ingredients include Bauer’s simple zeros, Montgomery’s zero density, Kozma and Oravecz’s compact real-frequency construction, the connected-set theorem of Fenchel, and the prime-gap theorem of Baker, Harman and Pintz.
