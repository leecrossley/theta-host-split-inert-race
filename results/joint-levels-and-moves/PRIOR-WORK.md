## Deadlines for a common doubling

The original joint theorem gives arbitrarily late comparisons, a 5.6552 margin for the difference of the two normalized moves, and common-start representations on an unrestricted tail. The single-field waiting theorem gives explicit deadlines for prescribed gains and losses on 547/727 by combining finite Dirichlet zeros, a positive logarithmic window and a signed bound on ideal squares. The present extension puts both fields on the same doubling with a deadline after every starting height, checks every mixed harmonic in a product of two independently phased positive tests, and certifies finite common windows on the actual prime scores. [Original joint release](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/joint-levels-and-moves-v1.0.0) · [Single-field waiting bound](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/theta-race-waiting-bound-v1.1.0)

The Fejér polynomial, Logan kernel and connected-set refinement of Carathéodory's theorem are classical. Kaczorowski and Pintz proved the general oscillation theorem on power intervals. The new power-interval application checks which poles can survive in a fixed contrast and supplies a numerical margin in its almost periodic alternative. ## A fixed window for every target direction

Earlier versions of this paper proved the unrestricted-tail ellipse product, the finite common-start certificates, and a height-dependent power deadline for smaller sets. For a *fixed projection* they also located larger contrasts in each fixed power interval. The present result puts the complete earlier ellipse product, and a larger dyadic move square, in one bounded multiplicative interval after every height. Its quantifier is uniform over the directions of separation. Individual factors depending on a direction do not by themselves provide such a factor for the whole hull. [Earlier tagged paper](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/joint-levels-and-moves-v1.1.2)

The companion single-field paper builds positive filters for off-line zeros. Its spectral tilt supplies a nonnumerical fixed factor for a scalar move and an explicit polylogarithmic deadline at each height. Here shared zeros have vector residues, projections can cancel as the height varies, and several distinct real exponents may share one ordinate. The additional steps use a finite spanning set of residues, ordinary Vandermonde inversion across translated averages, and bounds uniform both near and away from its orthogonal complement. The critical-line certificates and Fenchel’s connected-set theorem provide the stated numerical targets and the upper bounds on common starts. [Scalar method](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/theta-race-waiting-bound-v1.2.0) · [Bárány and Karasev](https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5942)

Martin and Ng study inclusivity and the support of limiting distributions of prime-counting races in progressions under hypotheses on their zeros. Fiorilli and Jouve give unconditional examples of persistent cumulative biases over number fields. The present problem asks what *shared positive averages of actual, sliding prime-ideal scores* attain after each prescribed height and how many common doublings they require. Montgomery’s unconditional density estimate in fixed right half-strips supplies the required summability. [Martin and Ng](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/papers/downloads/IPNR.pdf) · [Fiorilli and Jouve](https://numdam.org/articles/10.5802/jep.192/) · [Montgomery](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mat564)

## Three common starts

Bárány and Karasev explain the connected-set refinements of Carathéodory’s theorem. Douglas Derry obtains three points in four dimensions for a curve of real order four under a hyperplane-intersection condition. The present oscillating curves have repeated hyperplane crossings, and connectedness alone supplies four common observations in the earlier result. The simplex face argument here is an application of the classical Brouwer fixed-point theorem. The literature also gives simplex versions of the intermediate value theorem. [Bárány and Karasev](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00454-012-9439-z) · [Derry](https://doi.org/10.4153/CJM-1956-042-3) · [Intermediate value theorem for simplices](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0166864119304420)

The same timing row works in the initial prime counts and in the limiting calculation for each target cell. ## The quartic inverse and its resolvents

The [preceding edition](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/joint-levels-and-moves-v1.3.0) finds the anchored three-height solution and proves generic irreducibility of its quartic. A generically irreducible quartic can have several different transitive groups, including groups of order a power of two. Recovering the group, deciding the physical sheet on the full pair of bands, and identifying the two elliptic curves require additional information about this particular prime matrix.

Harris identifies geometric monodromy with the Galois group of an enumerative problem. Pakovich proves explicitly that a simply branched rational map of degree $`m`$ has monodromy $`S_m`$ and gives the genus of its off-diagonal ordered-pair curve. The passage from a four-sheeted map to its unordered pairs and their complementary partitions is the classical trigonal construction. Donagi and Smith describe the unramified double cover in section III.1.3. Fisher gives the binary-quartic invariants used to compute the elliptic $`j`$-invariant. These results account for the general group, genus and isogeny deductions once the hypotheses are established. [Harris](https://doi.org/10.1215/S0012-7094-79-04635-0) · [Pakovich, Theorems 2.2 and 2.4](https://www.math.bgu.ac.il/~pakovich/Publications/imax.pdf) · [Donagi and Smith](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF02392458) · [Fisher](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0610318)
