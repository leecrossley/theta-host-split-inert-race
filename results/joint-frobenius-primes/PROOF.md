# Proof of the two sharp prime-window thresholds

## Which primes and which traces

Set $`K_0=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{16})^+`$. The two theta hosts have conductor pairs $`(u,v)=(547,727),(571,599)`$, in that order. Their base fields are $`F=K_0(\sqrt{uv})`$ and their quadratic extensions are $`E=K_0(\sqrt{-u},\sqrt{-v})`$. Write $`M=16\cdot547\cdot727\cdot571\cdot599=2176229286416`$. Use ordinary primes $`p`$ coprime to $`M`$ that split in $`K_0`$, so $`p\equiv\pm1\bmod16`$. Remove also the four primes 343057911569, 343070636977, 413718278401 and 603339156001 omitted in the two original races. Every prime in the certificate is eligible on both fields. [Field and trace conventions](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/algebraic-heights-v1.0.0/results/algebraic-heights/PROOF.md) · [Base-field decomposition](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/joint-levels-and-moves-v1.2.0/results/joint-levels-and-moves/PROOF.md#reproducing-the-numerical-certificate)

For each conductor $`r`$, let $`s_r=(p/r)\in\{-1,1\}`$. Since $`r\equiv3\bmod4`$, quadratic reciprocity identifies this with $`(-r/p)`$. On each host there are eight degree-one primes above $`p`$ exactly when $`s_u=s_v`$. In that case they all split when the common sign is positive, and all are inert when it is negative. When the signs differ there is no degree-one term. Thus the split-positive sum of degree-one signs divided by eight is $`(s_u+s_v)/2`$. Write $`e(p)`$ for the pair of these normalized traces. The race’s opposite sign convention reverses both coordinates, which permutes the nine patterns. The four individual quadratic signs determine $`e(p)`$. An interior coordinate equal to zero hides which of its two signs was positive.

The complete four-sign label is the integer $`c=\sum_{j=0}^3 2^j\,\mathbf1_{s_j=+1}`$, with the conductors in the stated order. A trace label gives $`e(p)`$ itself. Each of its nonzero coordinates has one preimage pair of signs. A zero coordinate has two. The four corner trace values are the vertices of $`[-1,1]^2`$. The convex hull of available traces is the whole square exactly when all four vertices occur.

## The complete map of missed deadlines

Take real $`L\ge1`$ and $`C\ge4/3`$. For one state, suppose $`p,q`$ are consecutive eligible primes of that state. If $`p\le L\lt q`$, the next prime is $`q`$. The interval $`(L,CL]`$ therefore misses the state exactly when $`p\le L\lt q/C`$. For its first prime $`q`$, use the sentinel $`p=1`$. If $`q/p\le4/3`$, this deficient interval is empty for every admitted $`C`$. A finite certificate for all larger gaps gives the complete miss set at once, including irrational factors and real starts. ## Where the last two gaps close

The greatest right prime in any deficient trace gap is 4,831. The state is $`(+1,+1)`$ and the preceding prime is 2,879. Since $`3\cdot4831/4=14493/4`$, every start in $`[2879,14493/4)`$ lacks that corner. At the right endpoint the prime is included. All trace gaps with omitted intervals end by that height. This also gives the sharp permanent threshold for the four vertices and for the convex hull. For the individual corners $`(-,-),(-,+),(+,-),(+,+)`$ the final right primes are respectively 1,999, 2,609, 3,727 and 4,831.

The greatest right prime for the sixteen signs is 8,737, following 6,047 in state 14, which has signs $`(-,+,+,+)`$. The missed starts in this last gap are exactly $`[6047,26211/4)`$. The corresponding averaged trace is $`(0,+1)`$. That trace can already be obtained from the other sign pattern $`(+,-,+,+)`$. Thus losing a particular sign can be invisible in the trace-pair data. These are thresholds beyond which *every* real start works. The complete table also includes earlier starts that work. [Exact gaps by state](evidence/wide-gaps.tsv)

For an all-height factor, each listed gap contributes its largest necessary factor at its left endpoint, namely $`q/p`$. The other gaps cost at most $`4/3`$. The three maxima for corners, traces and full signs are 337, 431 and 769. They are attained at $`L=1`$, by the first prime of $`(-1,+1)`$, of $`(0,+1)`$ and of sign pattern 8 respectively. The inclusive deadline makes each optimum attainable.

## Covering the finite range

Start with $`l_0=2^{24}`$ and $`l_{j+1}=\lfloor13l_j/10\rfloor`$. In every bin take one eligible prime of each of the sixteen types between $`l_j`$ and $`65l_j/64`$, inclusive. End with the first $`l_j\ge2^{1585}`$. There are 4,126 bins and 66,016 certified primes. Their formal bin limits imply a ratio at most $`(13/10)(65/64)=169/128\lt4/3`$ between successive selected primes of each type. The replay checks the actual ratios as integers, including the connection from the last prime below $`2^{24}`$ of each type. The small-prime sieve reaches past that first bin. The last selected prime in every type exceeds $`e^{1097}`$, since $`1585\log2\gt1097`$. A chain $`p_0,p_1,\dots`$ with $`3p_{i+1}\le4p_i`$ supplies a witness at every real $`p_i\le L\lt p_{i+1}`$. Intervening primes can only shorten a wait. The analytic estimate below takes over before any last witness is reached. ## Excluding the possible real zero

There are four real characters on the units modulo 16, induced by the primitive discriminants $`1,-4,8,-8`$. Each odd conductor prime has two real characters. The Chinese remainder theorem lists all primitive inducing discriminants as $`D=d\prod_{r\in I}(-r)`$, where $`I`$ is any subset of the four primes. Exclude the principal choice. These 63 discriminants are distinct and fundamental. Their conductors are $`q=|D|`$. At positive real arguments each omitted Euler factor of an induced character is nonzero. The principal primitive factor has no real zero on $`[127/128,1]`$, by the alternating series for zeta on the open side and its pole at 1.

For the other factors the computation proves $`L(\sigma,\chi_D)\gt0.288`$ throughout that closed segment. Put $`A(t)=\sum_{n\le t}\chi_D(n)`$. For positive real $`\sigma`$, partial summation gives $`L(\sigma,\chi_D)=\sigma\int_1^\infty A(t)t^{-1-\sigma}\,dt`$. A uniform bound for its tail follows from finite Fourier inversion. At each nonzero frequency the Fourier coefficient of the primitive character has absolute value at most $`\sqrt q`$, and at frequency zero it vanishes. The finite geometric sum at frequency $`j`$ has absolute value at most $`1/|\sin(\pi j/q)|`$. Pairing opposite frequencies and using $`\sin(\pi j/q)\ge2j/q`$ for $`1\le j\le q/2`$ gives $`|A(t)|\le\sqrt q(1+\log q)`$. The single midpoint for even $`q`$ can be overcounted. This argument holds for arbitrary real $`t`$ by taking its integer part. [Explicit character-sum estimates](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.05114)

Cut the integral at $`N=2^{26}`$. Starting from $`l=1`$, use the next endpoint $`h=\min(N,l+\max(1,\lfloor l/192\rfloor))`$. Compute the signed integer minimum $`m`$ of $`A(n)`$ over $`l\le n\lt h`$. For $`k=0,1`$ cover $`1-(k+1)/256\le\sigma\le1-k/256`$. Write the integrand as $`t^{-2}A(t)\sigma t^{1-\sigma}`$. If $`m\ge0`$ multiply it by $`(255-k)l^{k/256}/256`$. If $`m\lt0`$ use $`(256-k)h^{(k+1)/256}/256`$. In both cases multiply the result by the exact integral $`(h-l)/(lh)`$. For the remaining tail subtract at most $`\sqrt q(1+\log q)N^{-1+(k+1)/256}`$.

The replay computes outward powers from eight directed integer square roots on a $`2^{-40}`$ grid. Each cell sum is rounded down. For the tail use the upper bound $`(\lfloor\sqrt q\rfloor+1)(1+0.7\,\mathrm{bitlength}(q))`$. The smallest certified lower bound is greater than 0.288 at $`D=-1088114643208`$. This uses 2,758 integration blocks per cell and two cells per character. The replay separately checks the initial partial sums by modular exponentiation. ## All sixteen patterns in shrinking intervals

Bordignon’s Theorems 1.1 (Kadiri’s zero-free region) and 1.2 isolate a possible real exceptional zero with $`\beta\ge1-1/(6.3970\log M)\gt127/128`$. The preceding check rules it out. Exactly $`\varphi(M)/64`$ reduced classes have any prescribed four signs and $`p\equiv\pm1\bmod16`$. Indeed two of the eight units modulo 16 qualify and half the units at each of the four prime factors have a given sign. Let $`\Theta_c(x)`$ sum $`\log p`$ over those primes through $`x`$, temporarily retaining the four finite host omissions. They disappear from every interval considered here because its lower endpoint exceeds them. [Bordignon, Theorems 1.1 and 1.2](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2021/27-54v.pdf#page=2) · [Journal Table 6](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2021/27-54v.pdf#page=21)

For $`t=\log x\ge1097`$ use the published row $`(Y_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,C)=(7,5,1,2.83)`$. The conditions are $`M\le t^5`$ and $`e^7\lt1097`$. Summing the von Mangoldt errors over the selected classes gives at most $`(2.83/64)x/t`$. Removing all proper prime powers costs at most $`\sqrt{x}\,t^2/\log2`$. There are at most $`\sqrt{x}`$ possible bases for each of at most $`t/\log2`$ exponents, each with weight at most $`t`$. This is below $`0.0005x/t`$, since $`t^3e^{-t/2}`$ decreases there. We obtain $`|\Theta_c(x)-x/64|\lt0.045x/t`$.

Write $`U=L+\Delta`$ with $`\Delta\gt0`$ and $`t=\log L\ge1097`$. The error in interval mass about $`\Delta/64`$ is less than $`0.045(2L+\Delta)/t`$. For $`U=4L/3`$, the ratio $`(2L+\Delta)/\Delta`$ is 7. Thus the interval mass of every sign pattern lies strictly between $`\Delta/66=L/198`$ and $`\Delta/62=L/186`$. For any $`\Delta\ge6L/t`$ the lower bound is greater than $`\Delta/1800`$. Dividing any lower mass bound by $`\log U`$ gives the corresponding lower bound for the number of distinct primes. In particular the positive mass above $`e^{1097}`$ completes the sharp threshold proof.

For $`t\ge6004`$ the published row $`(Y_0,\alpha_1,\alpha_2,C)=(8.7,5,10,0.019)`$ applies since $`e^{8.7}\lt6004`$ and $`M\lt6004^5`$. The prime-power contribution is below $`10^{-7}x/t^{10}`$ because $`t^{12}e^{-t/2}`$ decreases in this range. Put $`E=0.019/64+10^{-7}`$. The interval error is at most $`E(2L+\Delta)/t^{10}`$. For $`\Delta\ge kL/t^{10}`$ the mass divided by $`\Delta`$ differs from $`1/64`$ by at most $`E(2/k+1/t^{10})`$. At $`k=1`$ the mass lies strictly between $`\Delta/67`$ and $`\Delta/61`$. At $`k=3`$ it lies strictly between $`\Delta/65`$ and $`\Delta/63`$. [Bordignon, Table 6](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2021/27-54v.pdf#page=21)

For $`t\ge12959`$ we also have $`M\le t^3`$ and $`e^{8.3}\lt t`$. The row $`(8.3,3,10,0.0032)`$ therefore gives the same endpoint estimate with $`E=0.0032/64+10^{-7}`$. Already at $`k=1`$ the mass lies strictly between $`\Delta/65`$ and $`\Delta/63`$. A trace pair with $`j`$ zero coordinates has $`2^j`$ preimages, so these bounds are multiplied by $`2^j`$ for that pair. The replay checks each comparison with rational arithmetic.
