# Prior work and scope

Reviewed 21 November 2026. This result varies the integer exponent in the dilation $`y\mapsto 2^ny`$ on the 547/727 field. It proves a two-sided oscillation theorem for all of the first billion exponents, a billion-exponent separation between possible exceptions anywhere, and a positive-density subset on which all sufficiently high starts give uniformly small moves. I found no earlier publication of these three bounds for this race.

| Source | Relation |
|---|---|
| Fixed shifts on the same field | The short-shift theorem takes any fixed width and a fixed move up to a doubling, with bounds on the change divided by the log distance. The fixed-factor theorem supplies a deadline for finding both signs for a doubling. Neither covers integer powers through a billion or gives spacing or density bounds in the exponent. [Short shifts](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-short-shifts/blob/483c31f2df2132ed2165620ea55e20e969228bfb/PROOF.md) · [Waiting factor](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-fixed-factor/blob/12b7c94cfb9586c25fb29e7c7aaf0a20adaf5b2f/PROOF.md) |
| One-way windows and joint recurrence | The window certificate supplies the 6,000 sign brackets, the even compact moment and the pointwise dyadic error. The synchronized-window theorem fixes the dilation at two and varies the starting height. Here the bounded quantity is the change over an entire power of two at *every* high start. The finite separation proof uses two distinct conjugate frequency pairs, an exhaustive integer cover and a uniform norm triangle inequality. [Window theorem](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-factor-400/blob/f1773b07a55e76947d4e0c6ab19a112fd91fe6cf/PROOF.md) · [Paired density](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-paired-density/blob/5181e3a8545c67f1bc93f344b39683cab4a10383/PROOF.md) |
| Almost periodic prime errors | Kaczorowski and Ramaré show how almost periodicity enters prime-number errors. Akbary, Ng and Shahabi develop limiting distributions and mean-square methods for error terms. The qualitative recurrence mechanism and Fourier coefficient inequality are classical. The arithmetic inputs here yield explicit bounds in a different variable. [Kaczorowski and Ramaré](https://ramare-olivier.github.io/Maths/kaczo.pdf) · [Akbary, Ng and Shahabi](https://www.cs.uleth.ca/~nathanng/RESEARCH/AkbaryNgShahabi.pdf) |
| Return sets of circle rotations | Positive lower density for simultaneous returns to a box is classical. In this proof finite pigeonholing finds a bounded multiple of any prescribed exponent, and triangular Fourier kernels give the lower density even in the presence of resonances. [Chapman and Chow, discussion of polynomial Bohr sets](https://research-information.bris.ac.uk/files/404074869/GenRadoRoth_FINAL.pdf) |
| Primitive Dirichlet zeros | The published field-specific profile assumes GRH for the primitive Dirichlet functions, including the base field. Alpöge's announced, unrefereed Dirichlet GRH theorem supplies this input. Positive multiplicities remain in every coefficient bound. [Pinned theorem](https://github.com/leanprover-community/riemann-hypothesis/blob/371a1a26d5e17f604428e3a8ce6ce341cf517434/src/Main.lean#L12) [Kernel and statement checks](https://math.mit.edu/~drew/grh/) |

The search covered the field primes and radicand, dyadic exponents and prime-ideal races, uniform small shifts of prime-number errors, almost periods, integer dilation powers, the billion-exponent threshold, simultaneous approximation and rotation returns. A public code search combined the field pair with “dyadic powers”. This assesses the identifiable literature and public certificates. It cannot settle priority over unpublished or unindexed work.
