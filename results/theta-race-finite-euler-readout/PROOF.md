# Inverting the finite 547/727 race

## The observable

Let $`K=\mathbf Q(\zeta_{16})^+`$, $`F=K(\sqrt{547\cdot727})`$, and $`E=K(\sqrt{-547},\sqrt{-727})`$. The quadratic extension $`E/F`$ assigns a sign $`\epsilon_v=1`$ to a split prime ideal and $`-1`$ to an inert one. We use the good places in the theta frame, omitting rational primes $`2,547,727,343057911569,343070636977`$. For each good place the product of the normalized first-step theta-trace jumps above it equals $`\epsilon_v`$. The preceding construction proves this for the concrete field and all good places. [Field and admissible frame](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/main/results/theta-race-short-shifts/SecondField.lean) · [Fibre trace identity](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/main/results/theta-race-short-shifts/RaceTrace.lean#L83)

Put $`B=2\sqrt2(\sqrt2-1)^2`$. The original normalised race is $`S(y)`$. Its unnormalised value and its mass at each integer norm are

```math
A(y)=B\sqrt y\,S(y)=\sum_{n\ge1}c_nW(n/y),\qquad
c_n=-\sum_{Nv=n}\epsilon_v\log n,\qquad
W(t)=\max(0,\min(2-2/t,\,4/t-1)).
```

Every sum at a finite height has finite support. Set $`a_k=A(k/4)`$ for positive integers $`k`$ and define $`a_0=0`$. Write $`D_k=a_{k+1}-2a_k+a_{k-1}`$. For every good odd prime $`p`$ the number

```math
u(p)=-\frac{pD_p}{\log p}
```

is the first power sum of the eight local character values. Together with the order of $`p`$ in $`(\mathbf Z/16\mathbf Z)^\times/\{\pm1\}`$, it determines their entire Euler denominator. Any three approximate raw samples, each with error strictly smaller than $`(\log p)/p`$, determine the same answer by rounding to the nearest of $`-8,0,8`$. All three samples see only norms through $`p`$. For example, at 31 the first two raw samples vanish and the third is $`8\log(31)/31`$. Hence $`u(31)=-8`$. ## The three knots and the inverse at every norm

The proof here applies to every real sequence $`(c_n)_{n\ge1}`$. At integer tick $`k`$ the contribution of a single unit mass of norm $`n`$ is

```math
W(4n/k)=\frac{(k-n)_+-\tfrac32(k-2n)_++\tfrac12(k-4n)_+}{n},\qquad x_+=\max(0,x).
```

Indeed the slopes in $`k`$ are successively $`0,1/n,-1/(2n),0`$. This identity extends to $`k=0`$ by the value zero. The centered second difference of $`(k-m)_+`$ is one at $`k=m`$ and zero at every other integer. Summing the resulting three terms, with $`c_x=0`$ for noninteger indices, gives

```math
e_k:=kD_k=c_k-3c_{k/2}+2c_{k/4}.
```

For odd $`k`$ this isolates $`c_k`$. For $`k=2^vm`$ with $`m`$ odd, the inverse on the whole finite dyadic chain is

```math
c_k=\sum_{j=0}^{v}(2^{j+1}-1)e_{k/2^j}.
```

To verify the inverse let $`L`$ act by halving the index, with value zero off the positive integers. On the chain, $`e=(1-L)(1-2L)c`$ and $`L^{v+1}`$ vanishes at its last index $`k`$. Multiplying the two finite geometric inverses gives coefficient $`1+2+\cdots+2^j=2^{j+1}-1`$. In the odd case, the highest sampled tick is $`p+1`$. Positive weight requires $`n\lt p+1`$, so even the last sample uses only norms through $`p`$. For a freely variable mass at $`p`$, all observations up to and including height $`p/4`$ are independent of that mass, since its weight first becomes positive to the right. ## Which Euler factor is recovered

Write $`\xi`$ for the primitive even quartic character modulo 16. The factors of $`\zeta_E/\zeta_F`$ correspond to $`\chi_{547}\xi^j`$ and $`\chi_{727}\xi^j`$ for $`j=0,1,2,3`$, at their primitive conductors. Here $`\chi_q(n)=(n/q)`$. At a good odd rational prime set $`s=\chi_{547}(p)`$, $`t=\chi_{727}(p)`$ and $`z=\xi(p)`$. Frobenius in $`E/\mathbf Q`$ has components $`(s,t,z)`$, and complex conjugation is $`(-1,-1,1)`$. Degree-one places in $`F`$ exist precisely when $`z=1`$ and $`s=t`$. In this case all eight places have sign $`s`$. In every other case the prime has no base ideal of norm $`p`$. Thus

```math
-c_p/\log p=(s+t)(1+z+z^2+z^3)=u(p).
```

For $`d`$ the order of $`p`$ modulo 16 up to sign, $`z`$ has order $`d`$. Let $`D_p(T)`$ denote the product of $`1-\chi(p)T`$ over the eight primitive characters, so the local Euler factor is $`1/D_p(p^{-w})`$ in the complex variable $`w`$. Multiplication by a sign permutes the full subgroup generated by $`z`$ when $`d=2`$ or $`4`$. When $`d=1`$ there are four copies of each of $`s,t`$. These facts give the complete table. [Galois group and eight factors](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/main/results/theta-race-short-shifts/PROOF.md)

| Order $`d`$ | Measured $`u(p)`$ | Denominator $`D_p(T)`$ |
|---|---|---|
| $`4`$ | $`0`$ | $`(1-T^4)^2`$ |
| $`2`$ | $`0`$ | $`(1-T^2)^4`$ |
| $`1`$ | $`8`$ | $`(1-T)^8`$ |
| $`1`$ | $`-8`$ | $`(1+T)^8`$ |
| $`1`$ | $`0`$ | $`(1-T^2)^4`$ |

For the five omitted primes the raw race assigns no mass at any of their powers. Their Euler denominators follow separately from the primitive conductors. At two only the two odd-conductor quadratic characters survive, with values $`-1`$ and $`1`$, giving $`1-T^2`$. At 547, the four surviving twists have quartic component of order four, giving $`1-T^4`$. At 727 it has order two, giving $`(1-T^2)^2`$. Both auxiliary type primes are congruent to one modulo $`16\cdot547\cdot727`$ and therefore have denominator $`(1-T)^8`$. ## Precision and verification

If each raw sample has absolute error at most $`\delta`$, the error of $`D_k`$ is at most $`4\delta`$. At a good odd prime the induced error in $`u(p)`$ is at most $`4p\delta/\log p`$. With $`\delta\lt(\log p)/p`$ this is strictly below half the gap between successive allowed values. For the scalar nearest-value rule at a prime of order one, the open endpoint is sharp in the independent additive-error model. Errors of signs $`+,-,+`$ and magnitude $`(\log p)/p`$ move the decoded scalar by exactly four. The opposite signs move it the other way. The midpoint is compatible with either adjacent scalar value under that error model. For the general inverse, the absolute error in $`c_k`$ is at most

```math
4k\delta\sum_{j=0}^{v}(2-2^{-j})=4k(2v+2^{-v})\delta .
```

Errors here are in $`A(y)`$. An error bound in $`S(y)`$ must first be multiplied by the known $`B\sqrt y`$ at its sample height. [Lean noise and rounding lemmas](FiniteReadout.lean)

The score generator sieves rational primes and uses the finite field arithmetic. At a good rational prime $`q`$ let $`f`$ be its residue degree in $`F`$, the least positive integer with $`\xi(q)^f=1`$ and $`(\chi_{547}(q)\chi_{727}(q))^f=1`$. There are $`8/f`$ ideals of norm $`q^f`$, of common sign $`\chi_{547}(q)^f`$. Their combined mass is $`-8\chi_{547}(q)^f\log q`$. Prefix sums of the masses and of mass divided by norm evaluate both affine branches at every requested tick. Arb encloses each trace before it is rounded. The program checks the digest of all ordered denominator rows. [Score calculation](decode.py) · [Complete table digest](evidence/factor-digest.txt)

A second sieve and the eight primitive Conrey labels compute the ordered factors directly. For the archived examples through 1009 the second program also factors the defining quartic and both defining quadratics over each residue field and sums the three weights term by term. It checks the trace from all sixteen archived triples, including 999983. A separate rational computation exhausts the single-mass differences through norm 256 and recovers a signed sequence of that length. Six local lemmas compile and replay in the Lean kernel. The Galois and reconstruction arguments above use only finite field and real arithmetic after the cited trace identification.
