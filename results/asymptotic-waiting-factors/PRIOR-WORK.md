# Prior work

Kozma and Oravecz proved in Theorem 1 that a real trigonometric polynomial on a torus with a finite symmetric integer spectrum has a zero in every closed ball of a specified diameter. On a one-dimensional torus with angular frequencies $`\gamma=2\pi k`$ their diameter is exactly $`\pi\sum_{\gamma\gt0}1/\gamma`$. Their proof eliminates frequencies by positive translated sums. This supplies the historical precedent for the diameter and the positive filter in the present argument. A single sinusoid gives the familiar half-period threshold. [Kozma and Oravecz, 2006](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0601021)

Eremenko and Novikov prove a lower bound for the density of sign changes of real Fourier integrals with a spectral gap under stated growth hypotheses. Density of individual sign changes in an initial interval allows long gaps. Here the observables are two prime-only doubling moves, both of prescribed amplitude, in the same local window. Kaczorowski and Pintz’s Theorem (1.7) localizes arithmetic oscillations in power intervals. Schlage-Puchta localizes large absolute values of the ordinary Chebyshev error using a zero of zeta. These precedents concern related oscillation phenomena and different quantifiers. [Eremenko and Novikov](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0301060) · [Kaczorowski and Pintz](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/1987.Kaczorowski_2.pdf) · [Schlage-Puchta](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.00853)

Montgomery’s fixed-half-strip zero-density estimate makes the reciprocal sum of the ordinates absolutely convergent off the critical line. Platt supplies the rigorous exclusion of off-line zeros at low heights for the required conductors. Uniform recurrence of an absolutely convergent trigonometric series and polynomial interpolation at finitely many exponents are classical. These are inputs to the proof. [Montgomery](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/mat564) · [The density estimate as stated by Inoue](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/jtnb.1162.pdf#page=11) · [Platt, Theorem 7.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.3087)

The preceding result in this family establishes one unconditional fixed factor for every height. The earlier factor-400 paper gives a specific large-height miss assuming Dirichlet GRH, which Alpöge's announced theorem supplies, together with recurrence under the indicated critical-line inputs. The sparse-grid paper characterizes an off-line zero by power growth sampled on fine observation grids. The related delayed-difference criterion concerns error bounds for general Dirichlet families. None of those statements tests one fixed amplitude in two nearly coincident **actual doublings**, or characterizes an unattained extremal abscissa by success after every sufficiently late height for every factor above two. [Fixed-factor result](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/theta-race-waiting-bound-v1.2.0) · [Factor-400 certificate](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/theta-race-factor-400-v1.1.0) · [Sparse-grid theorem](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/compact-filter-oscillation-v1.0.0) · [Delayed-difference criterion](https://github.com/leecrossley/prime-window-rh-criterion/tree/main/results/dirichlet-window-transfer)

The new conclusions here use the support budget on a complex, possibly unattained spectral edge. A signed probe placed in a positive part of the measure retains one complex coefficient and handles different real parts at the same ordinate. It gives both strict power-sized prime moves in each eligible window, the 14,488-ordinate necessity for repeated factor-400 misses, the short-reversal equivalences, and an unconditional positive-density result for a pair completing by 2.011H. The last computation reuses the previously published even gamma kernel at two new arguments. The comparison covered trigonometric zero gaps, spectral-gap sign-change theorems, localized arithmetic oscillation, prime-window criteria, and the earlier field-specific certificates. No earlier statement of these results for this field was located. This is a scoped priority comparison.

## The optimal amplitude at factor four

The even prime-race profile, its positive coefficients, its expression in terms of the gamma kernel, and its limiting upper level had already been established in this family. Earlier curvature papers computed the supremum of a symmetric second difference for a prescribed separation. The earlier dyadic word catalogue gave simultaneous examples of successive gains and losses. The preceding version of this result compared two short factors at the much smaller common amplitude 1.9735. Each is a precursor to the new optimization over **every** pair of eligible starts, with the smaller of two opposite signed changes as objective. [Earlier level maximum](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-isolated-peaks/releases/tag/v1.0.0) · [Symmetric second differences](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-width-cusp/releases/tag/v1.0.0) · [Six-doubling catalogue](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/consecutive-doubling-patterns-v1.0.0) · [Earlier reversal criterion](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/asymptotic-waiting-factors-v1.0.0)

The usual spectral Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and almost-periodic recurrence are classical. The decisive field arithmetic is monotonicity of the exact even profile on the full interval from zero through $`\log 2`$. It makes the upper bound valid for every overlapping or disjoint eligible pair and makes it sharp at the limiting separation. The explicit two-deadline comparison uses both a lower witness and a global upper bound.
