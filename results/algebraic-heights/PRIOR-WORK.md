It leaves open how many independent square roots the different hits require. The 547/727 factor-18 result computes the first loss and several signed threshold intervals by two prime-sum methods. Here the knot sweep and direct tent-prefix logic build on those evaluations. The present calculation classifies all positive target magnitudes through one million on both hosts, and the two checkers reconstruct and separate the branch constants. The infinite argument uses Baker’s theorem, the prime number theorem in fixed progressions, recurrence of the signed scores, and the nonzero four-knot coefficient calculation. ## Equal amplitudes at different dilations

The previous crossing paper isolates 62 individual heights and bounds the number of labelled hits in an algebraic-ratio class. Its one-cell construction gives algebraic-ratio pairs at amplitudes next to zero. The present argument excludes signed identities at **every nontrivial algebraic dilation** by comparing the product of a single prime’s slope and intercept. It gives the countable exceptional set on both fields at every positive height, and integer-ratio examples in every subinterval of the guaranteed amplitude range. On the arithmetic side the 72-place certificates apply to individual pairs even when the earlier counting theorem cannot identify which pair has transcendental quotient. [Preceding ratio theorem](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/releases/tag/algebraic-heights-v1.2.0) · [Baker’s independence theorem](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0025579300003971)

On the log-height axis the condition for equal signed values on one field is a horizontal chord. The classical universal chord theorem and Hopf’s characterization study possible chord lengths of a continuous function on a fixed interval. Davis and Troubetzkoy prove a lower bound on the measure of available lengths. Those results supply the natural comparison for the interpolation argument. The number-theoretic statements here prescribe an amplitude band, two fields and signs, arbitrarily late starts and consecutive **integer dilations** at positive lower natural density. They also locate the attainable amplitudes in a countable field-theoretic set. The count of prime knots controls how many ratio intervals can lose integer starts at their endpoints. [Burns, Davidovich and Davis on Hopf’s theorem](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.00871) · [Davis and Troubetzkoy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.12820)
