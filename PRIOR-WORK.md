# Prior work and scope

Checked on 21 November 2026. The claim under review is the explicit endpoint $e^{162000}H^{3001/2999}$, valid after every starting height for **both** signed dyadic changes of magnitude greater than $1.9735\log 2$ in the 547/727 split–inert prime-ideal score. The direct $18H$ certificate through one million concerns the same observable. No earlier statement or proof of this quantitative bound surfaced in the sources reviewed. Unpublished results and gaps in indexing limit any claim of priority.

| Source | Comparison |
|---|---|
| The earlier 547/727 short-shift theorem | The field, trace identification, weight, normalization and threshold all come from this release. Its nonnegative trigonometric products and limiting Abel means prove arbitrarily late gains and losses. The limiting mean supplies no deadline after a prescribed $H$. Here a compact test supported after that $H$ gives the deadline and includes the endpoints of both moves. [Previous theorem and proof](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-race-short-shifts/blob/483c31f2df2132ed2165620ea55e20e969228bfb/PROOF.md) |
| Alpöge and the contemporaneous host notes | The original parameter recursion supplies the trace increment used in the earlier release. The analytic window assembly in the original source estimates a different prime sum on supplied arithmetic windows and varies auxiliary data. Revision 37 of bramble_trace's reading guide includes a finite-height adverse-zero box for an ordinary-zeta tent, a finite positivity test through one million, and a note about paired cutoffs. Those arguments concern an ordinary centred ramp or tower windows. The present field is fixed and we seek two opposite changes of a prime-ideal score after **every** height. The revision archive has SHA-256 f42701b5015f7068ca0ef04d49e7e91702a6062ac686acd9c0709261c70fa709. | Knapowski and Turán | Theorem 1.3 gives a sign change on a specified range after a large starting point for differences of cumulative rational progression $\psi$-counts under a low-lying zero exclusion. It establishes a precedent for effective waiting bounds in prime races. A sign change in a cumulative count does not locate either threshold crossing of a difference of two normalized moving windows. [Comparative Prime-Number Theory VII](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/1963.Knapowski_7.pdf) |
| Ingham, Anderson–Stark, Best–Trudgian | Transfer of a transform singularity to an oscillation by positive trigonometric tests is classical. Numerical applications also certify sets of favourable ordinates. This proof isolates one known zero with a compact average whose order grows with the starting log height and bounds the unselected spectrum uniformly in that height. [Ingham](https://www.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/1942.Ingham.pdf) · [Best–Trudgian on the Anderson–Stark method](https://arxiv.org/abs/1209.3843) |
| Pintz and Révész | The programme of deducing quantitative oscillation from one zero predates this calculation. Révész treats the magnitude of the cumulative remainder in the Beurling prime number theorem and discusses localized estimates from known zeros. The prime-only dyadic difference here has an additional base-field square spectrum, two signed tests, a specified coefficient and an explicit endpoint valid at every height. [Révész, especially Theorems 1 and 2 and the localization discussion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2202.01837) |
| Lehman-type crossover bounds | A positive smoothed explicit formula with verified low zeros and rigorous upper bounds for the rest can force a prime-counting inequality on a bounded interval. Saouter, Trudgian and Demichel refine this approach for positive values of $\pi(x)-\operatorname{li}(x)$ near a selected centre. Here every starting height supplies a search interval and both signs must occur within it. [Saouter–Trudgian–Demichel](https://openresearch-repository.anu.edu.au/bitstreams/3bbcd8d2-860d-434a-8708-eb7c12e33986/download) |
| Grenié–Molteni and Platt | The symmetric kernel has a precursor in Grenié and Molteni's class-group estimates. Platt's Theorem 7.1 establishes the finite-height Dirichlet input used here. The ordinate itself lies within earlier coverage. The contribution here is its isolation for this trace, the displacement multiplier, the two complex-disk exclusions for prime squares, and the quantitative use of the unchecked spectrum. [Kernel, Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.02430#page=6) · [Platt, Theorem 7.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3087#page=14) |
| Other races | Fiorilli–Jouve obtain unconditional eventual dominance for certain cumulative conjugacy-class counts. Leung studies moving-interval distributions under RH and linear independence. A contemporaneous weighted rational-prime repository studies the effect of power weights on race densities. None gives the stated interval for this field and score. [Fiorilli–Jouve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12245) · [Leung](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04000) · [Weighted rational-prime races](https://github.com/raghavs1729/weighted-prime-races) |
