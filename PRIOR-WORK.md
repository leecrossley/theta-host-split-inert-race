# Prior work and scope

Reviewed on 21 November 2026. The priority claim concerns inequality (1) for the 547/727 trace race, including its numerical constant, all moves up to a doubling, the derivative endpoint and compact positive mixtures. The five-ordinate certificate and construction of an admissible datum on this field are part of the result. The literature and code reviewed below yielded no earlier publication of that bound. Priority across all unpublished or unindexed work remains open.

The named records `H0` and `H1` both use 571/599. The smaller 11/7 field example supplies a different set of field hypotheses. Here the 547/727 pair, both type primes, both distinguished split primes and the norm-prime witness give a concrete rank-compatible record and admissible character datum. | Earlier 571/599 release | The generic fibre identity is reused verbatim. The earlier theorem bounds the level of one window. A large limsup and a small liminf alone give no control over increments at a fixed log distance. For example, $\sin(\log(2+x))$ on the log-scale variable $x$ has both extreme levels and tends to change arbitrarily slowly on every fixed-length interval. On the new field we bound short increments via their imaginary residues and the common signs of their sine multipliers. Equation (2) is the direct field specialization of the earlier level criterion. [Earlier theorem and criterion](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/1434f28e894b9d5060a41d711b5e43f8bc8e53ca/PROOF.md) · [Original fibre proof](https://github.com/leecrossley/theta-host-split-inert-race/blob/1434f28e894b9d5060a41d711b5e43f8bc8e53ca/FiberTrace.lean) |
| Ingham, Diamond, Anderson–Stark, Best–Trudgian | Extracting oscillations from transform residues, using positive trigonometric tests and checking bounded relations between zero ordinates are classical. Applied to a single translated-difference transform, these methods already explain why such a test can work. We supply the eight exact factors, the certified exclusions and the uniform multiplier estimate for the entire family of differences, including the derivative and mixtures. [Ingham](https://www.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/1942.Ingham.pdf) · [Diamond](https://personal.math.ubc.ca/~gerg/teaching/592-Fall2018/papers/1971.Diamond.pdf) · [Best–Trudgian, Theorem 2 and the Anderson–Stark criterion](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1209.3843#page=3) |
| Grenié and Molteni | Their work on class-group generators supplies the exponential autocorrelation underlying this centred kernel. They also give explicit estimates under GRH for integrated prime-ideal counts. These works credit the earlier smoothing and distinguish their estimates from a two-sided lower bound on the local motion of a signed splitting race. [Class-group kernel, Theorem 3.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1607.02430#page=6) · [Smoothed prime-ideal estimates](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.4465) |
| Leung | The closely related short-interval race compares rational-prime counts at multiple locations and proves distribution results under RH and independence conditions. Here the counted objects are base prime ideals in a fixed CM splitting race. A five-zero certificate gives an unconditional lower bound on both directions of its log-scale slope. [Joint distribution of primes in multiple short intervals](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.04000) |
| Splitting and comparative races | Aoki and Koyama study cumulative, weighted Chebyshev bias against splitting and its relation to the deep Riemann hypothesis. Fiorilli and Jouve prove unconditional eventual dominance for certain number-field races. Martin and Ng study inclusivity for cumulative residue-class races. These results concern different statistics or give different types of conclusion. [Aoki–Koyama](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.12266) · [Fiorilli–Jouve](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.12245) · [Martin–Ng](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.00088) |
| Platt | Platt's finite-height calculations cover the primitive conductors in this note, all at most 11632. The underlying Dirichlet zeros and the finite-height method have prior numerical coverage. We separately enclose five sign changes, check signed combinations of their ordinates against every factor, and certify how they add in the shifted statistic. [Theorems 7.1 and 7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.3087#page=14) |
